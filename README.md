## 🏀 Overview

**Precision Playbook** is a next-generation finals shooting simulator crafted for elite performance trainers, sports academies, and competitive coaching staff who demand granular control over every rep, release, and rebound. Where the original concept focused on capturing the rhythm of a single shooter, Precision Playbook zooms out and treats the entire training environment as a living, breathing organism — one where every variable, from crowd noise decibels to the arc of a wrist flick, can be tuned, tracked, and mastered.

Think of it as a maestro's baton for the hardwood. Instead of a simple shot chart, you get a rehearsal stage. Instead of static numbers, you get a story told through latency, biomechanics, and decision fatigue. This repository is the heartbeat of that stage.

The simulator is engineered around a resilience-first architecture: it performs gracefully whether you're running a laptop courtside at a regional final or a full workstation in a performance lab. Every session becomes a data artifact, and every artifact becomes a coaching insight.

## 🚀 Getting Started (Guided Setup)

Precision Playbook is designed so that even a coach with zero coding background can be up and running within a single practice session. The onboarding flow walks you through environment selection, athlete profile creation, and calibration in a matter of minutes.

For teams deploying across multiple devices, a shared configuration file synchronizes settings so every workstation behaves identically. Detailed configuration guides live in the `/docs` directory of the repository.

## 🏗️ Project Structure

The repository is organized into modular layers, each responsible for a distinct slice of the training experience. A core simulation engine drives shot physics, while an atmosphere module governs crowd audio and visual pressure. Analytics and reporting live in their own wing, keeping the codebase approachable for contributors who specialize in one area.

Documentation files, changelogs, and roadmap notes are kept in clearly labeled directories so trainers and developers can each find what they need without wading through unrelated material.
